Project 01 · Activation · RBI card statistics, Jan–Sep 2026

Banks are issuing credit cards faster than people use them

India's credit card base kept growing through 2026, but spend per card did not keep pace. This project sizes the gap with RBI data and proposes a first-90-days activation journey that a card issuer could ship.

What the data shows

  • Issuance is steady. Cards outstanding rose from 116.65 million in January to 125.30 million in September 2026, up 7.4% in nine months.
  • Usage is not. Monthly spend per card fell from ₹17,145 in January to ₹16,281 in September. Apart from the March year-end spike, it has stayed below ₹17,000 every month since April.
  • The year-on-year picture is sharper. September 2026 spend fell 5.9% from a year earlier, the first annual decline since February 2021, while average spend per card fell 14.8% (RBI data via Moneycontrol).
  • Issuers differ a lot. In April 2026 an HDFC Bank card averaged ₹21,977 of spend, 30% more than an ICICI Bank card at ₹16,927.

Reading this carefully. Card spend is seasonal. March carries year-end corporate and tax payments, and in 2026 part of the festive spending moved from September into October. That is why I compare the trend across months and the same month year on year, not one month against the next.

The user problem

Persona: Neha, 26, salaried, Pune. She accepted a lifetime-free card through an app offer. She pays for groceries, auto rides and chai by scanning UPI QR codes from her debit account. The card stays in her wallet for the occasional online sale. She doesn't know her reward rate and worries about missing a due date.

For the bank, Neha is a cost: acquisition spend, a credit bureau pull and a card that earns almost nothing. RBI's 2022 card directions also mean a card left unactivated for 30 days needs her fresh consent to stay open, so the first weeks decide whether she becomes a customer at all.

The product: a first-90-days activation journey

FeatureWhat it doesMetric it moves
First-spend nudgeIn-app and SMS prompt within 7 days of delivery, tied to a small welcome cashback on the first three spends.% of cards with a spend in 30 days
Pick-your-category welcome offerNeha chooses groceries, fuel or food delivery for boosted rewards in month one, so the card fits a habit she already has.Transactions per active card
Small-ticket railWhere the issuer offers a RuPay variant, one-tap linking of the card to UPI for QR payments.Share of everyday spend on card
Plain-language first billA first statement that explains due date, minimum due and interest in three lines, plus two reminders before the due date.Early delinquency, complaint rate

As a new cardholder, I want to know what my card is good for in my first week, so that I use it for something I already buy.

As a new cardholder, I want a clear reminder before my first due date, so that I never pay a late fee by accident.

Success metrics

  • North star: average spend per card in the first 90 days.
  • Input metrics: 30-day activation rate, transactions per active card, share of cards with three or more spends in month one.
  • Guardrails: 90-day delinquency rate, complaints per 10,000 new cards, reward cost per activated card.

Risks and trade-offs

  • Over-nudging annoys customers and can look like pushing debt. Cap messages and let customers opt out in one tap.
  • Welcome rewards attract cashback hunters who churn. Tie the offer to three spends across separate days, not one large spend.
  • Spend growth that comes with higher delinquency is not a win. The guardrails above are reviewed weekly with the credit risk team.

Discovery plan

The data shows the gap; it doesn't show why. Before building anything, I would talk to 10 people who got a credit card in the last 12 months and rarely use it.

I believeHow I'll test itWhat would change my mind

Interview guide · 20 minutes

  1. Screener: got a credit card in the last 12 months; used it fewer than 3 times last month.
  2. How did you end up with this card? Who suggested it?
  3. Walk me through the last 10 things you paid for. How did you pay each time?
  4. When did you last use the card? What made you pick it that time?
  5. What do you get for using this card? (Don't prompt.)
  6. What, if anything, worries you about using it?
  7. What would make you reach for it at a grocery shop?
  8. If the bank sent you one message this week, what would actually be useful?

Method

I compiled monthly cards outstanding and total spend for January–September 2026 from RBI's bank-wise card statistics as reported in the press, and calculated spend per card and a January-based index. The data and the script are in this folder (data.csv, issuers_apr2026.csv, analysis.py), each row with its source link. The same numbers are queried in SQL in the data workbench.
